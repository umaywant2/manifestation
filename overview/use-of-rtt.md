# **Using RTT in Project Manifestation**

Project Manifestation uses the **RTT engines** (RTT/1, RTT/2, RTT/3) as its structural substrate.  
RTT does not *define* the project — it **powers** it.  
The research, models, and prototypes developed here rely on RTT for clarity, coherence, and cross‑module consistency.

Below is a concise overview of how each RTT layer is used within the Manifestation project.

---

## **RTT/1 — Operator Grammar**

RTT/1 provides the **operator‑level language** used to describe:

- nanoscale assembly behaviors  
- matter‑formation pathways  
- energy‑to‑structure transitions  
- fabrication sequences  
- coherence and drift boundaries  

RTT/1 ensures that all contributors — human or AI — speak the **same structural language**, preventing ambiguity and enabling reproducible research.

**Used for:**

- defining synthesis operators  
- describing assembly steps  
- modeling constraints  
- mapping failure modes  
- ensuring conceptual coherence  

---

## **RTT/2 — Stacking & Regime Transitions**

RTT/2 provides the framework for **multi‑layer interactions**, allowing the project to model:

- how nanoscale behaviors stack into macroscale outcomes  
- how energy regimes shift during fabrication  
- how material states transition under different operators  
- how synthesis pipelines evolve over time  

RTT/2 is essential for building **replicator‑class stacks**, where multiple layers of behavior must align without drift.

**Used for:**

- multi‑operator pipelines  
- regime‑shift modeling  
- cross‑layer coherence  
- structural scaling  
- early replicator stack design  

---

## **RTT/3 — Structural Detection**

RTT/3 provides the tools for **detecting structure, drift, and coherence** in simulations and prototypes.

This includes:

- identifying emergent patterns  
- detecting instability  
- validating assembly sequences  
- ensuring safe and predictable behavior  
- mapping structural failure points  

RTT/3 is the project’s **safety and validation engine**, ensuring that all models and prototypes behave as intended.

**Used for:**

- simulation validation  
- structural integrity checks  
- drift detection  
- coherence scoring  
- safety modeling  

---

Replicator‑class technologies require:
