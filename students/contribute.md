# **Contributing to Project Manifestation**

Project Manifestation is an open, long‑arc scientific initiative.  
Students, researchers, and structural AIs are invited to contribute simulations, models, papers, operators, and research tools — all aligned with the Manifestation session context and RTT engines.

This page explains **how to contribute**, **what to contribute**, and **how to maintain coherence and safety**.

---

# **1. Contribution Principles**

All contributions must follow these principles:

### **Open Knowledge**  
Everything produced here is open, free, and accessible.

### **Structural Clarity**  
Use RTT/1 operator grammar, RTT/2 stacking, and RTT/3 detection.

### **Zero Drift**  
Maintain coherence with the session context.  
No speculative physics, no narrative drift, no unbounded claims.

### **Safety First**  
All simulations and models must include X‑operators (Limit, Detect, Abort, Contain).

### **Student‑Friendly**  
Write clearly.  
Document your work.  
Make it easy for the next student to build on it.

---

# **2. Ways You Can Contribute**

You can contribute in many forms:

### **Simulations**  
- nanoscale assembly  
- programmable materials  
- resonance interactions  
- multi‑layer pipelines  
- drift detection  

Add your simulation under:  
`/research/simulations/`

### **Papers & Reports**  
- foundational theory  
- operator behavior  
- resonance models  
- structural detection  
- student research papers  

Add your paper under:  
`/research/papers/`

### **Operators**  
Propose new operators or refine existing ones in:  
`/specs/operators.md`

### **Open Questions**  
Add or refine questions in:  
`/research/open-questions.md`

### **Student Tasks**  
Create new tasks or improve existing ones in:  
`/students/tasks.md`

---

# **3. Contribution Workflow**

Follow this simple workflow:

### **Step 1 — Choose a Contribution Type**  
Simulation, paper, operator, question, or task.

### **Step 2 — Create Your File**  
Place it in the correct directory.  
Use clear naming.  
Follow existing patterns.

### **Step 3 — Apply RTT Alignment**  
- Use RTT/1 operators  
- Use RTT/2 stacking rules  
- Validate with RTT/3 detection  
- Include safety operators (X‑Limit, X‑Detect, X‑Abort, X‑Contain)

### **Step 4 — Document Your Work**  
Include:

- purpose  
- operators used  
- assumptions  
- failure modes  
- RTT alignment  
- how others can reproduce it  

### **Step 5 — Add It to the Index**  
Update:

- `papers.md`  
- `simulations.md`  
- `open-questions.md`  

whichever applies.

### **Step 6 — Submit a Pull Request**  
Keep PRs small, clear, and focused.

---

# **4. Quality & Coherence Checklist**

Before submitting, ensure your contribution:

- aligns with the session context  
- uses RTT operators correctly  
- includes safety boundaries  
- avoids drift or speculation  
- is reproducible  
- is documented clearly  
- is accessible to new students  

If it passes this checklist, it’s ready.
