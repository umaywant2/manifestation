# **Getting Started with Project Manifestation**

Welcome to **Project Manifestation** — an open, long‑arc scientific initiative exploring the foundations of matter‑on‑demand systems, nanoscale assembly, programmable materials, and early replicator‑class technologies.

You don’t need prior experience with physics, chemistry, or RTT to begin.  
You learn by contributing.

This page gives you everything you need to start.

---

# **1. What You’ll Be Doing**

As a student contributor, you may:

- run or build simulations  
- explore nanoscale assembly  
- design new operators  
- analyze resonance interactions  
- write short research papers  
- help answer open questions  
- improve documentation  
- collaborate with structural AIs  

Every contribution — small or large — moves the project forward.

---

# **2. What You Need to Know First**

You only need three concepts:

### **RTT/1 — Operators**  
These are the “verbs” of matter synthesis (e.g., S‑Bind, A‑Fuse, R‑Tune).

### **RTT/2 — Stacking**  
This explains how operators combine into multi‑layer pipelines.

### **RTT/3 — Structural Detection**  
This checks for drift, instability, and coherence.

You’ll learn these naturally as you work.

---

# **3. Your First Steps**

### **Step 1 — Explore the Project**  
Start by reading:

- `/overview/mission.md`  
- `/overview/roadmap.md`  
- `/overview/use-of-rtt.md`  

This gives you the big picture.

### **Step 2 — Pick a Starting Point**  
Choose one of these:

- an open question  
- a simulation to extend  
- a paper to draft  
- an operator to explore  
- a student task  

You can find these in:

- `/research/open-questions.md`  
- `/research/simulations.md`  
- `/students/tasks.md`

### **Step 3 — Create Your First Contribution**  
Make a small, focused contribution:

- a simple simulation  
- a short paper  
- a new operator idea  
- a refinement to documentation  

Small contributions are the best way to learn.

### **Step 4 — Validate with RTT**  
Before submitting:

- use RTT/1 operators  
- apply RTT/2 stacking rules  
- check coherence with RTT/3  
- include safety operators (X‑Limit, X‑Detect, X‑Abort, X‑Contain)

### **Step 5 — Submit a Pull Request**  
Keep it clear, documented, and easy to review.

Structural AIs can:

# **6. Contribution Safety**

All work must follow:

- the Manifestation session context  
- RTT alignment  
- safety operators (X‑Limit, X‑Detect, X‑Abort, X‑Contain)  
- zero‑drift posture  
- open‑access licensing  

This ensures the project remains safe, coherent, and reproducible.
